# The Flathead Fair — A Visitor's Guide

You already know the forest path that runs east and west through a small clearing. In fair season a packed public road leaves **northeast** from that same clearing toward meadow ground. The older routes are unchanged. You may ignore the new road entirely and still finish every business the Great Underground Empire has always required of you.

The Flathead Fair Association charges no general admission. If you go, you go as a visitor, not as a paying pilgrim.

## What sort of place this is

The grounds are a day's outing: a midway, food, games, a market, pavilions, rides, a fishing pond, an office that also serves as prize hall, a back lane, and a house of mirrors. People work there. They also walk about, close stalls, open others, and leave when the lanterns go out.

You may eat, play, ride, fish, dance, ask questions, or merely watch. You may spend a handful of coins or none. You may stay until dusk or turn around at the gate. The white house, the thief, the troll, and the rest of Zork will wait. They have waited longer than fairs.

A free **handbill** is usually stacked at the entrance. Read it. It is not a map, and it is not a dare.

## Coin of two kinds

Food, rides, and most games want **zorkmids**. Win a booth and you may be given **prize tickets**. Tickets are not money. They will not buy an elephant ear. Nell Harrow redeems them at the Prize Hall, which shares a building with the current fair office.

Ada Vellum keeps this fair's program, permits, and incident intake. Older programs live in the House of Records, where they have always lived. Do not expect the office desk to fetch the archive, or the archive to sell you candy.

A worn pouch sometimes lies near the northeast road. Twelve ordinary coins, if you want them. The Fair will also let you in with empty pockets.

## Hours, more or less

The day moves. Wagons wait before opening. Oil and painted signs reach the road once the gates are public. At dusk the Dance Pavilion sets **The Lantern Table**: supper, hot drinks, conversation, and no drink minimum. Later still, the lanterns go out and the road is only a way home.

If a booth is shuttered, a ride refuses the wind, or a familiar face has gone to another row, come back later. Looking twice is not a sin in Zork. It is often the whole method.

## Commands worth trying

The parser here is the parser you already use. A few words earn their keep on the meadow:

```text
> NORTHEAST
> READ HANDBILL
> ASK MABEL ABOUT FOOD
> BUY ELEPHANT EAR
> WATCH TARGET GALLERY
> RIDE OBSERVATION WHEEL
> FISH
> WEIGH FISH
> REDEEM STUFFED GRUE
> DANCE
```

`PLAY`, `EXAMINE`, `SMELL`, `GIVE`, `ASK`, and `TELL` remain as useful as they were west of the house. `BUY`, `RIDE`, `FISH`, `WEIGH`, `WATCH`, `REDEEM`, and `DANCE` are the extra manners of the Fair. Silas Dace will discuss a rod if you mean to fish. Watch a booth before you play it. The people who work a stall usually know their own stall.

Do not expect a complete catalogue of mounts, fillings, or prizes. The handbill is short on purpose.

## Looking, asking, waiting

The Fair rewards the same habits as the rest of Zork. Examine what the paragraph named. Ask the person who would actually know. Watch a mechanism before you put money on it. Return when the light changes.

Some games are distinct from one another. Observation is part of play at more than one of them. The House of Mirrors is a walk, not a pamphlet. Fishing is optional and real.

If you meet Mara, she is one person with preferences, errands, and the right to refuse. She is not a prize, a meter, or a hint sheet.

## You do not have to go

The Fair is a meadow with a gate. Canonical Zork remains playable if you never take the northeast road. You may spend a day among frying oil and gilt lies about Frobozz, or you may leave the clearing by the old east path toward the canyon and never smell cinnamon-sugar again.

Either is a complete visit to the underground.
