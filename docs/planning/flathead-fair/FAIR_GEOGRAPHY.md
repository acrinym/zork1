# Flathead Fair geography

**Status:** OPEN

## Geography law

The fair must attach to real Highly Extended Zork geography without stealing or rerouting canonical exits. The player can ignore it completely.

The fair should be compact and dense rather than enormous and hollow. Initial target: roughly **12–18 major fair locations**, with attraction interiors only where the activity genuinely benefits from them.

## Working district families

- Fair Entrance / ticket and information area
- Central Midway
- Food Row
- Games Row
- Market / craft stalls
- Grand Pavilion / performance area
- Dance pavilion or evening social pavilion
- Observation wheel
- Carousel / ride area
- Fishing pond and pond path
- House of Mirrors
- Agricultural / exhibition area if retained by lore pass
- Prize pavilion
- quieter edge/lookout space
- service/backstage edge
- after-hours routes that become meaningful when crowds leave

## Density standard

Every major room should support multiple verbs, people, objects, or events. Avoid `north-south` filler.

## Privacy gradient

The geography should naturally create different social contexts:

- crowded public midway;
- semi-public pavilion seating;
- ride-carriage privacy;
- pond path / overlook quiet;
- backstage or closed-ground spaces after hours.

Romantic or private behavior should arise from actual location context rather than arbitrary `ROMANTIC-ROOM` flags.

## Canonical protection

- Existing solutions remain available.
- Fair closure cannot strand the player on the wrong side of a canonical route.
- No required treasure is relocated into the fair.
- No canonical room permanently becomes inaccessible because a fair tent occupies it.

## Open decision

The exact attachment point to the Zork map is intentionally unresolved and must be chosen only after examining geography, travel time, weather exposure, and the fair's cultural origin together.
