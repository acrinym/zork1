# Flathead Fair miniquests, incidents, and rumors

**Status:** STABLE FOR FIRST STORY-WEB PLANNING  
**Rule:** discovered through the world, not dumped into a universal quest log

## Story law

A fair story begins because the player **notices something, hears something, asks a question, finds an object, enters a contest, or witnesses an incident**.

The narrator does not announce `NEW QUEST: RETRIEVE THE SUGAR CRATE`.

Stories may resolve with a physical object, information, a record/ribbon, zorkmids/tickets where natural, vendor/NPC memory, Mara memory/reaction, access/new questions, a changed world fact, a joke, or an intentionally unresolved contradiction.

When a story creates documentary evidence, current-fair paperwork begins at the Fair Office. Historically meaningful durable evidence may later compose the existing upstairs Hall/House of Records under `FAIR_HALL_OF_RECORDS_INTEGRATION.md`.

## Initial story web

### F-01 — The Crate That Went to the Wrong Fair

**Primary NPC:** Mabel Rusk  
**Locations:** Food Row -> Back Lane -> Exhibition Yard / Market Row  
**Type:** mundane help / commerce / physical-object story

#### Discovery

Mabel is short one marked supply crate after setup. She does not immediately deputize the Adventurer. The player can discover this by asking why one menu item is unavailable, overhearing Mabel arguing about delivery, noticing an empty marked space among her supplies, or asking about a delayed batch.

#### Evidence trail

- Back Lane has delivery marks/people who saw where the cart went;
- the crate has a concrete vendor/contents marking;
- it was misdelivered to an adjacent exhibition/merchant load, not stolen by author fiat;
- witnesses provide only what they actually observed.

#### Resolution

Return/direct the real crate to Mabel or establish where it is so staff can recover it.

Possible reward: a free ordinary elephant ear / vendor goodwill / later recognition. If ignored, Mabel eventually resolves it through fair operations and loses sales/time. The fair does not freeze forever waiting for the player.

### F-02 — Thirty-Nine Tickets

**Primary NPC:** Nell Harrow  
**Location:** Fair Office & Prize Hall  
**Type:** social micro-story / choice

A young visitor has **39 tickets** and wants a 40-ticket prize, preferably something emotionally ridiculous such as a stuffed grue variant.

Nell's professional position remains: 39 is not 40.

The Adventurer may do nothing, give one transferable ticket, help choose another prize, ask whether a legitimate one-ticket activity is still open, or argue that 39 is “basically 40” and receive the response it deserves. 😂

There is no morality meter and no guaranteed romance reward. Mara may react according to what she actually witnesses and values.

The child/family NPC identity remains for the secondary-roster pass.

### F-03 — Ephraim Says the Date Is Wrong

**Primary NPCs:** Ephraim Peake, Ada Vellum  
**Locations:** Fair Office & Prize Hall, Grand Pavilion, upstairs Hall/House of Records, Pond Path/Fishing Pond  
**Type:** historical investigation / provenance / conflicting testimony

#### Premise

Ephraim insists a current official fair-history date is wrong.

Ada can establish what the **current Fair Office copy** says. She is not the master historical archive and cannot settle the matter merely by asserting “the records say so.”

#### Evidence sources

- current official program/permit/history wording held by Ada;
- Ephraim's lived memory;
- older physical programs, grounds maps or case files in the existing upstairs House of Records;
- an old sign/photo/object if one exists in an exhibit;
- an old stamped fair token that can very rarely be recovered from the fishing pond;
- Frobozz promotional material that may use a self-serving date.

#### Archive interaction

The player may need to go upstairs and use the existing physical archive/review/cross-reference authority rather than treating Ada as remote database access.

Source distinctions survive:

- `current Fair Office program claims X`;
- `archived older program prints Y`;
- `Ephraim remembers Z`;
- `Frobozz brochure claims W`.

#### Outcome philosophy

The player may establish that Ephraim is mistaken, discover that the current date refers to a reorganization rather than the first gathering, find evidence that Frobozz literature moved the founding date, or determine that evidence remains insufficient.

The system records **claims, evidence and provenance**, not an automatic quest-truth flag.

### F-04 — The Shell Game Is Not Merely Difficult

**Primary NPCs:** future dubious shell-game operator; Jonas Pell; Berrin Vale; Ada Vellum for current incident intake  
**Locations:** Games Row, Fair Office & Prize Hall  
**Type:** investigation / fair-play story

#### Baseline

Jonas provides an honest skill-game reference. The shell/cup game elsewhere appears beatable through observation but produces inconsistencies.

#### Evidence

Potential authored evidence includes marked/subtly different cups, a hidden transfer technique that violates stated rules, an object not where the public demonstration says it is, or repeated observation showing the operator changes method only when money is at stake.

The player can inspect, ask, watch without paying, compare with Jonas's explanation of fair-game practice, and present evidence.

#### Outcomes

- fail to establish cheating;
- confront the operator and get denial/deflection;
- show evidence to Berrin;
- file/trigger current incident intake through Ada where appropriate;
- recover a payment only if policy/evidence supports it;
- operator alters/closes the game or is sanctioned under authored association procedure.

If an incident is formally adjudicated and historically meaningful, the resulting current-office report becomes a candidate for later House of Records archival graduation. The old archive does not know about the incident before it happens.

No author cheat. If the booth is rigged, the rigging physically exists.

### F-05 — A Reflection That Is Late

**Primary NPCs:** Mara where present; Berrin/current Fair Office if reported; later maintenance specialist  
**Location:** House of Mirrors  
**Type:** Zork-weird / observation mystery

Most mirror behavior is ordinary optical trickery. In a narrow authored state, one reflection can lag an action by one beat.

The event distinguishes player misunderstanding of an angled sightline, another visitor causing an apparent mismatch, and a genuinely anomalous reflection event.

The player may repeat the action, inspect the frame/glass/floor/sightlines, ask Mara what she saw, show an object to the mirror, report it, or leave it unresolved.

A report is a **claim/report**, not automatic proof. If a later physical investigation produces durable evidence, that evidence can be archived through normal provenance rules. The anomaly does not become a monster fight or portal solely because it was noticed.

### F-06 — Lost Purse, Actual Owner

**Primary systems/NPCs:** Fair Entrance meeting point; Ada Vellum lost-and-found; crowd/minor visitor NPC  
**Locations:** Central Midway, Fair Entrance, Fair Office & Prize Hall  
**Type:** mundane help / contextual knowledge

The player finds a real purse/container in a plausible crowd location.

Important behavior:

- it has physical contents/identifying clues;
- taking it does not automatically mark it returned;
- Ada can intake it as **current lost property**;
- an owner can describe facts they should know;
- a wrong claimant may know only visible features;
- the player may keep it, return it, turn it in, ask around or ignore it according to broader property/consequence authority.

This is ordinary current Fair Office work. It does not become a permanent House of Records case merely because somebody lost a purse.

### F-07 — Orin's Missing Sheet

**Primary NPC:** Orin Bell  
**Locations:** Dance Pavilion, Grand Pavilion, Pond Path / Central Midway depending wind state  
**Type:** mundane incident / performance variation

A real sheet of music or set note is displaced during an authored setup/weather incident.

If the Adventurer notices and returns it, Orin can use the intended program. If not, the band **still performs** using a substitute piece/order. Missing the story does not freeze the evening.

The later performance and Orin's memory differ according to whether the sheet returned.

### F-08 — Wheel Closed for Wind

**Primary NPC:** Emery Wicks  
**Locations:** Observation Wheel, Central Midway / Fair Office only if refund escalation is needed  
**Type:** operational incident / commerce fairness

The player buys or intends to buy a wheel ride near a weather threshold.

Possible outcomes:

- Emery refuses sale before payment because wind is already unsafe;
- a paid but not-yet-boarded ride is suspended and refunded/credited;
- a ride underway completes under its authored safe-stop procedure;
- the wheel reopens if later weather permits.

This is useful precisely because nothing supernatural happens. It proves the fair has adults running machinery rather than author-controlled vending machines.

Routine weather closure/refund paperwork normally remains current operational noise rather than permanent archive material. A truly significant incident could graduate only through the explicit records boundary.

### F-09 — The Pond Gives Back Something Old

**Primary NPCs:** Silas Dace, Ada Vellum, Ephraim Peake, Vera Tallow; existing House of Records  
**Locations:** Fishing Pond, Fair Office & Prize Hall, Market Row, upstairs Hall/House of Records  
**Type:** rare fishing discovery / provenance puzzle

A rare authored fishing result is an old stamped fair token or small object.

Different sources know different things:

- Silas knows whether similar things have been caught before;
- Ada knows current symbols/records and can identify what current Fair Office material says;
- Ephraim may claim to remember the style;
- Vera may identify manufacturing/material clues while separating knowledge from salesmanship;
- archived old programs, site maps or concession rolls upstairs may provide documentary context.

The player can keep it, show it around, sell it if ordinary ownership/commerce permits, or use it as evidence in F-03.

The House of Records does not magically identify the object. It provides specific archived sources that may corroborate, contradict or fail to mention it.

One physical object can therefore connect fishing, market knowledge and historical investigation without being replaced by an archive flag.

## Story interconnection law

Miniquests may share real objects/knowledge without becoming a dependency maze.

Examples:

- the old pond token can strengthen F-03 but is not required to enjoy fishing;
- Jonas's honest game can help reason about F-04 without a detective stat;
- weather that causes F-07 can also alter ride/fishing operation because it is the same weather state;
- Ada's Fair Office handles current lost property, entries and incident intake through one coherent current-service role;
- the existing House of Records handles historical documentary research rather than duplicating Ada's office.

Do not create duplicate “quest versions” of people, objects or records.

## Rumor and evidence model

Rumors/claims are attributed sources:

- vendor gossip;
- old-timer memory;
- child exaggeration;
- employee complaint;
- official current announcement;
- archived official document;
- Frobozz marketing;
- observed fact.

The game remembers **who or what said/recorded something** when truth is uncertain. Archive provenance/confidence/contradiction semantics should be reused where applicable.

## Incident model

Incidents may happen and resolve without the player's involvement.

Examples:

- a ride pauses;
- rain drives crowds under Grand Pavilion;
- a dropped food tray creates brief commotion;
- a performer substitutes a piece;
- a vendor sells out;
- a child loses something and later recovers it through staff even if the Adventurer does nothing.

The fair keeps living when the player walks away.

## Rewards

Not every story needs money or tickets.

Initial reward families include product/gift, returned property and recognition, vendor familiarity, historical knowledge, changed performance, fair-association response, record/ribbon, Mara/NPC memory, a physical curiosity, and a good line of dialogue.

## Failure and abandonment

Leaving a story incomplete has authored meaning:

- some resolve through staff;
- some remain unresolved;
- some opportunities pass;
- physical lost objects remain where world state puts them;
- nobody waits forever in a frozen quest pose because the protagonist went adventuring.

## Boundaries

- No universal quest log requirement.
- No generated errands.
- No `+5 GOOD PERSON` morality score.
- No random anomaly generator.
- No quest-only duplicate objects.
- No duplicate fair historical archive.
- No private Mara/NPC memory automatically becomes institutional paperwork.
- No fair story may be required for canonical Zork completion.
