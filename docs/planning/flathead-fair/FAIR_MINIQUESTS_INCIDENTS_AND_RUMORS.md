# Flathead Fair miniquests, incidents, and rumors

**Status:** STABLE FOR FIRST STORY-WEB PLANNING  
**Rule:** discovered through the world, not dumped into a universal quest log

## Story law

A fair story should begin because the player **notices something, hears something, asks a question, finds an object, enters a contest, or witnesses an incident**.

The narrator does not announce `NEW QUEST: RETRIEVE THE SUGAR CRATE`.

Stories may resolve with:

- a physical object;
- information;
- a record/ribbon;
- zorkmids/tickets only where natural;
- vendor/NPC memory;
- Mara memory/reaction;
- access or a new question;
- a changed world fact;
- a joke;
- an intentionally unresolved contradiction.

## Initial story web

### F-01 — The Crate That Went to the Wrong Fair

**Primary NPC:** Mabel Rusk  
**Locations:** Food Row -> Back Lane -> Exhibition Yard / Market Row  
**Type:** mundane help / commerce / physical-object story

#### Discovery

Mabel is short one marked supply crate after setup. She does not immediately deputize the Adventurer. The player can discover this by:

- asking why one menu item is unavailable;
- overhearing Mabel arguing about the delivery;
- noticing an empty marked space among her supplies;
- asking about a delayed batch.

#### Evidence trail

- Back Lane has delivery marks/people who saw where the cart went;
- the crate has a concrete vendor/contents marking;
- it was misdelivered to an adjacent exhibition/merchant load, not stolen by author fiat;
- a helpful or unhelpful NPC can provide only what they actually witnessed.

#### Resolution

Return/direct the real crate to Mabel or establish where it is so staff can recover it.

Possible reward: a free ordinary elephant ear / vendor goodwill / later recognition. The exact reward is less important than restoring stock and creating a memory.

If ignored, Mabel eventually resolves it through fair operations and loses sales/time. The fair does not freeze forever waiting for the player.

### F-02 — Thirty-Nine Tickets

**Primary NPC:** Nell Harrow  
**Location:** Prize & Records Hall  
**Type:** social micro-story / choice

A young visitor has **39 tickets** and wants a 40-ticket prize, preferably something emotionally ridiculous such as a stuffed grue variant.

Nell's professional position remains: 39 is not 40.

The Adventurer may:

- do nothing;
- give one of their own tickets if transferable under prize rules;
- help the visitor understand another prize choice;
- ask Nell whether any legitimate one-ticket activity is still open;
- attempt to argue that 39 is “basically 40,” receiving the response it deserves. 😂

There is no morality meter and no guaranteed romance reward. Mara may react according to what she witnesses and values.

The child/family NPC identity remains to be added in the next secondary-roster pass.

### F-03 — Ephraim Says the Date Is Wrong

**Primary NPCs:** Ephraim Peake, Ada Vellum  
**Locations:** Prize & Records Hall, Grand Pavilion, Pond Path/Fishing Pond  
**Type:** historical investigation / conflicting testimony

#### Premise

Ephraim insists an official fair-history date is wrong. Ada can show what the archive actually says. Neither person's statement automatically becomes objective truth.

#### Evidence sources

- official programs/permit records held by Ada;
- Ephraim's lived memory;
- an old sign/photo/object if one exists in an exhibit;
- an old stamped fair token that can very rarely be recovered from the fishing pond under the fishing authority;
- Frobozz promotional material that may use a different date for self-serving reasons.

#### Outcome philosophy

The player may establish:

- Ephraim is mistaken;
- the archive date refers to a reorganization rather than the first gathering;
- Frobozz literature quietly moved the founding date;
- evidence is insufficient to settle it.

The system records **claims and evidence**, not an automatic quest-truth flag.

### F-04 — The Shell Game Is Not Merely Difficult

**Primary NPCs:** future dubious shell-game operator; Jonas Pell; Berrin Vale/Ada Vellum if evidence is escalated  
**Locations:** Games Row, Prize & Records Hall  
**Type:** investigation / fair-play story

#### Baseline

Jonas provides an honest skill-game reference. The shell/cup game elsewhere appears beatable through observation but produces inconsistencies.

#### Evidence

Potential authored evidence includes:

- marked or subtly different cups;
- a hidden transfer technique that violates the stated rules;
- an object that is not where the public demonstration says it is;
- repeated observation showing the operator changes method only when money is at stake.

The player can inspect, ask, watch without paying, compare with Jonas's explanation of fair-game practice, and present evidence.

#### Outcomes

- fail to establish cheating;
- confront operator and get denial/deflection;
- show evidence to Berrin;
- recover a lost payment only if policy/evidence supports it;
- operator alters/closes the game or is sanctioned under authored association procedure.

No author cheat. If the booth is rigged, the rigging physically exists.

### F-05 — A Reflection That Is Late

**Primary NPCs:** Mara where present; Berrin/Ada if reported; later maintenance specialist  
**Location:** House of Mirrors  
**Type:** Zork-weird / observation mystery

Most mirror behavior is ordinary optical trickery. In a narrow authored state, one reflection can lag an action by one beat.

The event must distinguish:

- player failed to understand an angled sightline;
- another visitor caused an apparent mismatch;
- a genuinely anomalous reflection event.

The player may:

- repeat the action;
- inspect the frame/glass/floor/adjacent sightlines;
- ask Mara what she saw;
- show an object to the mirror;
- report it;
- leave it unresolved.

The anomaly does not become a monster fight or portal solely because the player noticed it.

### F-06 — Lost Purse, Actual Owner

**Primary systems/NPCs:** Fair Entrance meeting point; Ada Vellum lost-and-found; crowd/minor visitor NPC  
**Locations:** Central Midway, Fair Entrance, Prize & Records Hall  
**Type:** mundane help / contextual knowledge

The player finds a real purse/container in a plausible crowd location.

Important behavior:

- it has physical contents/identifying clues;
- taking it does not automatically mark it “returned”;
- Ada can intake it as lost property;
- an owner can describe facts they should know;
- a wrong claimant may know only visible features;
- the player may keep it, return it, turn it in, ask around or ignore it according to broader property/consequence authority.

This becomes a strong test of contextual questions: `WHAT'S IN YOUR PURSE?`, `WHERE DID YOU LOSE IT?`, etc., without creating a courtroom minigame.

### F-07 — Orin's Missing Sheet

**Primary NPC:** Orin Bell  
**Locations:** Dance Pavilion, Grand Pavilion, Pond Path / Central Midway depending wind state  
**Type:** mundane incident / performance variation

A real sheet of music or set note is displaced during an authored setup/weather incident.

If the Adventurer notices and returns it, Orin can use the intended program.

If not, the band **still performs** using a substitute piece/order. Missing the story does not freeze the evening.

The later performance and Orin's memory differ according to whether the sheet returned.

This is exactly the kind of small event that makes schedules matter without becoming a mandatory quest.

### F-08 — Wheel Closed for Wind

**Primary NPC:** Emery Wicks  
**Locations:** Observation Wheel, Central Midway / Prize Hall only if refund escalation is needed  
**Type:** operational incident / commerce fairness

The player buys or intends to buy a wheel ride near a weather threshold.

Possible outcomes:

- Emery refuses sale before payment because wind is already unsafe;
- a paid but not-yet-boarded ride is suspended and refunded/credited;
- a ride already underway completes under its authored safe-stop procedure;
- the wheel reopens if later weather state permits.

This incident is useful precisely because nothing supernatural happens. It proves the fair has adults running machinery rather than author-controlled vending machines.

### F-09 — The Pond Gives Back Something Old

**Primary NPCs:** Silas Dace, Ada Vellum, Ephraim Peake, possibly Vera Tallow  
**Locations:** Fishing Pond, Prize & Records Hall, Market Row  
**Type:** rare fishing discovery / provenance puzzle

A rare authored fishing result is an old stamped fair token or small object.

Different characters know different things:

- Silas knows whether such things have been caught before;
- Ada knows what official records/symbols say;
- Ephraim may claim to remember the style;
- Vera may identify manufacturing/material clues but must separate knowledge from salesmanship.

The player can keep it, show it around, sell it if ordinary ownership/commerce permits, or use it as evidence in F-03.

One physical object can therefore connect fishing, market knowledge and historical investigation.

## Story interconnection law

Miniquests may share real objects/knowledge without becoming a dependency maze.

Examples:

- the old pond token can strengthen F-03 but is not required to enjoy fishing;
- Jonas's honest game can help the player reason about F-04 but does not unlock a hidden detective stat;
- weather that causes F-07 can also alter ride/fishing operation because it is the same weather state;
- Ada's office can process lost property, records and complaints through one coherent institutional role.

Do not create duplicate “quest versions” of people or objects.

## Rumor model

Rumors are attributed claims.

Useful categories:

- vendor gossip;
- old-timer memory;
- child exaggeration;
- employee complaint;
- official announcement;
- Frobozz marketing;
- observed fact.

The game should remember who said something when truth is uncertain.

## Incident model

Incidents differ from miniquests because they may happen and resolve without the player's involvement.

Examples:

- a ride pauses;
- rain drives crowds under Grand Pavilion;
- a dropped food tray creates brief commotion;
- a performer substitutes a piece;
- a vendor sells out;
- a child loses something and later recovers it through staff even if the Adventurer does nothing.

The fair keeps living when the player walks away.

## Rewards

Not every story needs money or tickets.

Initial reward families:

- product/gift;
- returned property and recognition;
- vendor familiarity;
- historical knowledge;
- changed performance;
- fair-association response;
- record/ribbon;
- Mara/NPC memory;
- a physical curiosity;
- a good line of dialogue.

## Failure and abandonment

Leaving a story incomplete must have authored meaning:

- some resolve through staff;
- some remain unresolved;
- some opportunities pass;
- physical lost objects remain where world state puts them;
- nobody waits forever in a frozen quest pose because the protagonist went adventuring.

## Boundaries

- No universal quest log requirement.
- No generated errands.
- No `+5 GOOD PERSON` morality score.
- No random anomaly generator.
- No quest-only duplicate objects.
- No fair story may be required for canonical Zork completion.
