# Flathead Fair Mara experience

**Status:** STABLE FOR PLANNING  
**Scope:** fair-specific Mara preferences, initiative, refusals, competition and semantic memory  
**Boundary:** broader Mara autonomy, consent, relationship, property and rupture authority remains sovereign

## Goal

The fair should become one of the strongest places for the Adventurer and Mara to simply **live together for a while**. It must not become a relationship minigame.

Mara is a participant in the fair, not a companion bonus system. She can want something the Adventurer does not want, dislike an activity the Adventurer likes, wander toward something interesting, spend her own money when broader money authority supports it, win a contest, get bored, leave early, or decide she is having a very good time without converting that into automatic romance progress.

## Preference law

Mara's fair preferences are **authored character state**, not RNG.

They are stable baselines, but they are not robotic absolutes. Weather, fatigue, crowding, prior incidents, unresolved relationship tension, possession of real objects, and remembered prior fairs may change what she wants **in context**.

The distinction is:

- `Mara generally likes this` is character authority;
- `Mara wants this right now` is current world/relationship state.

The player does not automatically know every preference before Mara expresses or demonstrates it.

## Attraction preferences

### Observation Wheel — strong positive

The Observation Wheel is one of Mara's strongest fair preferences.

Baseline behavior:

- she especially likes it at **DUSK or EVENING** when the fair lights are visible;
- during daytime she may suggest waiting until later rather than spending the ride immediately;
- she values the view, changing light and quieter carriage context more than thrill-seeking;
- she can point out visible geography she actually knows;
- she may initiate conversation or ordinary affection when broader relationship/privacy authority supports it;
- she can still refuse because of unsafe wind, a bad queue, fatigue, unresolved tension or simply not wanting it at that moment.

A remembered good dusk ride can make her suggest the wheel again at a later fair. A remembered wind closure may make her ask Emery about conditions before paying.

### Carousel — playful positive

Mara enjoys the carousel primarily because the carved Zork-native creatures are ridiculous and specific, not because she needs a children's ride.

Her baseline first-choice mount is the **dragon** when available.

She may:

- inspect/compare figures before choosing;
- tease the Adventurer's mount choice;
- remember a prior mount;
- choose a different figure deliberately on a repeat visit;
- watch rather than ride if she is not in the mood.

The dragon preference is character flavor, not a stat bonus or mandatory couple ritual.

### Flying Chairs — conditional positive

Mara generally enjoys the motion and air of the Flying Chairs when conditions are comfortably safe, but it is not an attraction she wants to farm repeatedly.

She is more likely to accept/suggest it when:

- wind is ordinary rather than near a shutdown threshold;
- she has not just eaten heavily;
- the fair day still has enough energy for a faster ride.

She may refuse when wind is gusty, a loose carried/clothing object makes the situation annoying, she has already ridden it recently, or she is tired.

Her refusal is not a challenge for the player to overcome.

### Scenic Great Underground Empire Ride — curiosity/comedy positive

Mara likes the Scenic GUE ride most when she has enough real-world knowledge to notice that the attraction is sanitized, promotional or simply wrong.

She may:

- suggest it after learning more about a represented place;
- point out a discrepancy she personally knows;
- laugh at obvious Frobozz self-congratulation;
- ask the Adventurer what they remember;
- become less interested in immediate repetition unless later knowledge or a changed exhibit gives the ride something new.

The ride does not upload objective lore into her head. She can only challenge claims she plausibly knows are questionable.

### House of Mirrors — curious positive with repetition limit

Mara is curious about the House of Mirrors and usually enjoys a first pass.

Baseline behavior:

- she likes figuring out mundane sightline tricks;
- she may point out a physical navigation clue;
- she will play with ordinary distortions and reflections;
- if she witnesses a genuine authored anomaly, she is willing to recheck it **once or a small number of times** rather than becoming an infinite paranormal test harness;
- she may ask Hettie Bramm or staff about a mechanical explanation if the event matters enough;
- she dislikes pointless looping after the attraction has stopped yielding anything new;
- she strongly disapproves of breaking mirrors merely to see what happens unless broader circumstances provide an actual reason.

A strange reflection can become a personal memory without turning Mara into an anomaly oracle.

## Games Row preferences

Mara does not have one generic `LIKES GAMES` state.

### Strongest preferences

**What's Missing? Memory Table**

Mara enjoys the observation/memory challenge and may initiate a round. She can beat the Adventurer honestly. Repeated identical sets become boring once she has demonstrated mastery.

**Jonas Pell's Ring Stand**

She likes the fact that the equipment is understandable and Jonas explains the rules plainly. She may challenge the Adventurer, try a difficult target, or care more about an amusing prize than ticket efficiency.

### Moderate preferences

**Horseshoes / Ringed Stakes**

She enjoys a direct social challenge and can become competitive without needing a permanent skill stat.

**Clockwork Critter Race**

She likes watching the entrants and forming opinions about them. Any wager remains dependent on broader gambling/money authority. She can enjoy the race without betting.

**Clockwork Target Gallery**

She is willing to watch the cycle and try it, especially if the pattern can be learned. She does not want it reduced to repeated score grinding.

### Lower / situational interest

**Bottle Knockdown**

Ordinarily acceptable, but not a signature preference. A funny failure or exceptional clear can matter more than the booth itself.

**Flathead Bell Striker**

Mara is more interested in the spectacle/comedy and other people's attempts than in repeatedly proving force herself.

### Shell / Cup Game — skepticism becomes refusal

Mara may watch or try Kester Vane's booth before she has evidence that it is dishonest.

Once she personally sees enough inconsistency or is shown credible physical evidence, her baseline becomes:

- skeptical questioning;
- refusal to keep paying Kester merely to prove the same point again;
- possible interest in comparing the equipment or reporting the issue;
- annoyance if the Adventurer repeatedly feeds money into an obviously suspect booth after the matter is understood.

She does not magically know Kester cheats before she has a reason to know.

## Fishing — strong low-intensity preference

Mara genuinely likes fishing as a quiet fair activity rather than treating it only as a derby minigame.

Baseline preferences:

- preferred positions: **shaded bank** and **pond-path bend**;
- she is patient enough to wait through ordinary unsuccessful turns without demanding constant action;
- she prefers practical bait/gear choices over buying the most expensive option automatically;
- she may enter the derby when competition sounds fun, but does not need to enter every fair;
- she can submit a real catch and beat the Adventurer or Cassa Reed;
- she can lose to either without her personality collapsing into jealousy;
- she tends to prefer releasing an ordinary live catch after inspection/weighing unless there is a concrete reason to keep it.

She may become competitive with **Cassa Reed** because Cassa is a real rival angler rather than a hidden difficulty setting.

Mara's relationship state never changes fish probability.

## Food and drink preferences

### Elephant ears

Mara likes elephant ears. Her baseline favorite from the initial menu is the **apple-topped elephant ear (6 zm)**.

She still enjoys the cinnamon-sugar baseline and may choose it because it is simpler, cheaper or tied to a prior memory.

The oversized sharing elephant ear is not automatically `the couple option`. Mara may:

- suggest sharing it;
- prefer her own smaller one;
- accept or refuse a offered share;
- buy one herself;
- remember the entirely serious dispute over who ate most of a previous one. 😂

### Drinks

Mara likes **pear-lime fizz** as a daytime fair drink once she knows what it is, but it is not the only drink she will ever choose.

At DUSK/EVENING in cooler conditions, her baseline preference shifts toward **hot spiced cider** where available.

Product identity and Mara's knowledge remain separate. She cannot request `pear-lime fizz` by name before she has learned that the `large drink` is pear-lime fizz unless another legitimate knowledge source supplied that fact.

## Market and objects

Mara is interested in **Sella Birch's current craft** and **Vera Tallow's curiosities** for different reasons.

- Sella's stall appeals through workmanship, small carved objects and things that can become meaningful physical possessions.
- Vera's stall appeals through strangeness and provenance questions, but Mara does not automatically believe Vera's sales claims.

Mara values a **specific or funny meaningful object** more than a numerically expensive gift.

A small carved dragon from Sella, a ridiculous prize won through a shared incident, or a physical object tied to a real memory may matter more than the highest-ticket catalog item.

The Adventurer cannot force a gift into emotional significance merely by spending more zorkmids.

## Crowd preference and recovery

Mara enjoys the fair feeling alive. She is not written as automatically crowd-averse.

However, **MIDDAY peak crowd** is not her preferred state for hours of continuous wandering.

After sustained dense Midway/Games Row activity, she may suggest a lower-intensity move such as:

- Pond Path;
- fishing;
- Market Row;
- sitting/listening near a pavilion;
- waiting for DUSK before the wheel;
- eating somewhere less jammed.

This is contextual fatigue, not a visible `CROWD METER`.

## Music, dancing and evening

Mara likes the fair's DUSK/EVENING transition and Orin Bell's Dance Pavilion when the current relationship/context makes social engagement feel natural.

She may:

- notice the music starting and suggest going;
- ask the Adventurer to dance;
- accept or refuse being asked;
- remain for one piece and then leave;
- prefer a later Pond Path walk or wheel ride afterward;
- remember a particular song/dance at a later fair.

Dancing does not bypass unresolved relationship rupture, autonomy boundaries or consent. The fair never sets `DATE MODE` or `ROMANCE=TRUE` because music started.

## Phase-sensitive initiative

Mara's initiative should reflect what is actually open and what she has already done.

### PREOPEN

Normally no public-fair agenda. If legitimately present during setup, she is more likely to observe preparation than demand closed attractions.

### OPENING

Likely suggestions:

- walk the grounds before they become crowded;
- get something small to eat/drink;
- inspect Market Row;
- fish while the pond is quieter.

### LATE-MORNING

Likely suggestions:

- fishing;
- a favored game;
- Market Row;
- carousel / Ride Court while lines are manageable.

### MIDDAY

She may enjoy one high-energy activity but is more likely to suggest food or a move away from the densest repeated Midway loop after enough crowd exposure.

### AFTERNOON

Possible initiative:

- derby if entered;
- a competitive game;
- featured pavilion activity;
- checking a public result or prize actually earned.

### DUSK

This is one of her strongest initiative phases.

Likely suggestions:

- wait for / ride the Observation Wheel;
- get hot spiced cider if conditions fit;
- notice the lights coming on;
- move toward music.

### EVENING

Likely suggestions:

- Dance Pavilion;
- wheel if not already ridden;
- food/drink;
- Pond Path or quieter wandering after social activity.

### CLOSING

Mara may want one last legitimate activity, redeem something still redeemable, finish a conversation, or simply leave before the last possible moment.

She does **not** compulsively optimize every remaining attraction before closing.

### AFTER-HOURS

Mara does not treat closed fair infrastructure as a romantic playground. Legitimate presence follows real access/story authority.

## Refusal and annoyance law

There is no fair-specific relationship meter.

Mara's ordinary fair refusal should be understandable:

- `not now`;
- `I've had enough of that`;
- `I'd rather do X`;
- concern about weather/safety;
- hunger/fatigue;
- distrust of a booth;
- unresolved social/relationship context;
- simple preference.

A single repeated suggestion may sometimes be harmless or playful. Persistent repetition **after Mara has clearly refused or redirected** should feed broader autonomy/annoyance authority rather than resetting because the command was phrased differently.

Examples of behavior that should become annoying in context:

- repeatedly trying to drag her back into the same attraction after she says she is done;
- insisting on paying Kester again after she has said she believes the booth is rigged;
- turning a quiet fishing session into endless identical optimization attempts after she wants to leave;
- ignoring her expressed wish to eat, rest, walk somewhere quieter or go home.

The fair does not create a special punishment system for this. It composes existing Mara boundaries.

## Competition law

Mara is allowed to be good at things.

She can:

- beat the Adventurer at What's Missing, Ring Stand, horseshoes, fishing or another authored contest where mechanics support it;
- lose;
- improve through remembered experience where broader character memory supports it;
- challenge the Adventurer;
- challenge Cassa Reed;
- decline a rematch;
- care about a silly prize more than the official record.

No hidden romance-protection reroll makes the Adventurer win.

## Shared memories

High-value semantic memory candidates include:

- first fair together;
- first dusk/evening wheel ride;
- Mara's dragon carousel choice;
- first dance / a specific song;
- a ridiculous game failure or clean victory;
- Mara beating the Adventurer or Cassa in the derby;
- a large fish / record;
- a storm/rain/wind closure;
- staying through closing;
- Sella/Vera object bought or won;
- a particular elephant ear / drink choice;
- the shell-game investigation if Mara actually witnessed/participated;
- a genuine House-of-Mirrors anomaly if she actually saw it;
- the elephant-ear portion dispute. 😂

Memory should store the **semantic event and participants**, not a transcript.

Examples:

- `Mara remembers the dusk wheel ride where rain started on descent`;
- `Mara remembers she beat the Adventurer in the derby with a redfin bream`;
- `Mara remembers Kester's booth was sanctioned after she saw the waxed cup`.

A later fair can use that memory to change suggestions, recognition or dialogue.

## Public facts versus private lived memory

These remain separate.

A public fact may create documentary evidence:

- Mara wins a public derby, so a verified result can exist;
- she earns a public ribbon/record;
- she voluntarily gives a statement in an incident report.

The private experience around that fact remains personal:

- why she cared about winning;
- what she said afterward;
- affection in a wheel carriage;
- an argument on Pond Path;
- what a gift meant to her.

No automatic institutional surveillance.

See `FAIR_HALL_OF_RECORDS_INTEGRATION.md` and `FAIR_PERSISTENCE_AND_MEMORY.md`.

## Recurrence

Later fairs should let Mara remember selected prior experiences without becoming a transcript database.

Useful recurrence includes:

- suggesting the wheel near dusk because a prior ride mattered;
- recognizing Mabel, Tomas, Sella, Vera, Orin, Cassa or another NPC she actually met;
- remembering a real gift/prize still physically owned;
- changing her opinion after a bad/good prior experience;
- comparing a current record to a public result she remembers;
- noticing that an attraction or vendor changed;
- declining a repetition because `we already did that last time and I still don't like it`.

Annual recurrence does not require a fake global year counter.

## No DATE MODE

The player visits the fair with Mara through ordinary world actions. Relationship-appropriate behavior emerges from existing autonomy, consent, mood, memory, location, object and social authorities.

There is:

- no fair-only relationship mode;
- no automatic affection reward for completing attractions;
- no expensive-gift romance multiplier;
- no fair-specific consent bypass;
- no hidden difficulty adjustment to protect the player's ego;
- no institutional archive of private Mara/Adventurer life.
