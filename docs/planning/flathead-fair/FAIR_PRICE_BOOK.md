# Flathead Fair price book

**Status:** DESIGNING  
**Authority:** planning prices for ordinary fair commerce  
**Currency:** zorkmids (`zm`)

This file gives products actual economic meaning. Prices are not a universal economic simulator and do not imply that every merchant everywhere uses the same markup.

## General admission

- Fairgrounds entry: **FREE**
- Handbill / basic event sheet: **FREE** at entrance while supplies last
- Better printed souvenir guide: **1 zm**

Walking, talking, watching ordinary public activity, accompanying Mara, and using public paths do not require payment.

### Partial-identification example

The object initially presented as **`large drink`** is internally a **pear-lime fizz** costing **4 zm**.

A knowledgeable seller can answer:

> “Pear and lime. Mostly pear.”

Mara, a stranger, and the Adventurer may know less until smell, taste, signage, or conversation establishes it.

## Curiosity-market pricing

Curiosity merchants may price irrationally, haggle, misidentify, or speculate, but the item still has a concrete asking price at a given moment.

Working range:

- obvious junk: 1–5 zm;
- interesting old object: 5–25 zm;
- good puzzle/mechanism/collectible: 20–75 zm;
- rare specialist object: 75–250 zm;
- extraordinary object: 250+ zm.

The merchant saying an item is magical does not make it magical.

## Prize tickets

Prize tickets are not zorkmids and have no cash exchange rate.

Working redemption bands:

- 3–5 tickets: tiny novelty/prize sweet;
- 8–12 tickets: small toy, ribbon, whistle;
- 20–30 tickets: decent toy or modest souvenir;
- 40–60 tickets: stuffed creature / larger prize;
- 75–150 tickets: premium annual prize;
- major contest prizes: direct trophy/ribbon/object, not ticket arithmetic.

Ticket costs remain adjustable until the exact award rate of each game is designed. Do not create a grind economy where the rational path is to spam the cheapest booth indefinitely.

## Vendor variation

This price book defines fair-scale expectations, not identical menus. Examples:

- an artisan may charge more for hand work;
- an old regular may complain that elephant ears used to cost 2 zm;
- a late-night vendor may discount food near closing;
- a blatantly tourist-focused stall may overcharge;
- a vendor may give a friend, child, employee, Mara, or the Adventurer a contextual discount for an authored reason.

Any deviation should belong to a character/business fact rather than invisible difficulty scaling.
