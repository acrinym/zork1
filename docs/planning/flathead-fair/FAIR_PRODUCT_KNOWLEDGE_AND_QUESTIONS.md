# Flathead Fair product knowledge and questions

**Status:** DESIGNING

## Core law

A product's **actual identity** and the Adventurer's **current knowledge of that identity** are separate.

An object may initially be described as `a large drink`, `a red sweet`, `a fried thing`, or `a crooked brass object` while internally remaining a concrete authored product.

## Example knowledge progression

A drink may be:

- presentation: `large drink`;
- actual product: pear-lime fizz;
- vendor: knows ingredients, preparation, price, stock, and recommendation;
- Mara: may recognize pear by smell, learn exact flavor after tasting, or admit she does not know;
- random visitor: may know only where it was purchased;
- Adventurer: gains stable knowledge through examination, asking, smelling, tasting, reading signage, or prior memory.

The object does not change identity when discovered. The player's description/knowledge changes.

## Question intents that fair products should support where relevant

- What is this?
- What flavor is this?
- What's in it?
- Is it sweet / spicy / sour / hot / cold?
- How much is it?
- Who made it?
- Where did it come from?
- Is it fresh?
- Does it contain nuts or another notable ingredient?
- Have you tried it?
- Do you like it?
- What do you recommend?
- Why is it this color?
- Why is it moving?

Parser forms may vary; the authority is semantic rather than tied to one exact sentence pattern.

## No omniscient NPCs

A character answers only from knowledge they plausibly possess. A vendor can know their menu. Mara can know what she has observed/tasted/been told. A stranger may say they do not know and direct the player to someone who does.

Correct ignorance is valid authored dialogue. Generic ignorance when the NPC should know is not.

## Knowledge persistence

Meaningful product identification can persist for the current play history and, where existing memory authority supports it, across later encounters. Repeatedly asking a known question should not always pretend the information is new.
