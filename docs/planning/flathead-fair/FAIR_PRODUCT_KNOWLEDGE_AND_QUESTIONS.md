# Flathead Fair product knowledge and questions

**Status:** STABLE FOR PLANNING

## Core law

A product's **actual identity** and the Adventurer's **current knowledge of that identity** are separate.

An object may initially be described as `a large drink`, `a red sweet`, `a fried thing`, or `a crooked brass object` while internally remaining a concrete authored product.

This is not cosmetic flavor. Product knowledge is world state.

## Locked first example

The first acceptance example is:

- presentation: **`large drink`**;
- actual product: **pear-lime fizz**;
- price: **4 zm**;
- seller knowledge: exact flavor, ingredients, preparation, price, stock, and recommendations;
- seller's ordinary flavor answer: **pear and lime, mostly pear**;
- Mara knowledge: may recognize pear by smell, learn exact flavor after tasting/asking, or correctly say she does not yet know;
- random visitor: may know only where it was purchased or what they personally tasted;
- Adventurer: gains stable knowledge through examination, asking, smelling, tasting, reading signage, purchase context, or prior memory.

The physical object does not transform when identified. The player's description and knowledge change.

## Knowledge dimensions

A product may expose independently knowable facts such as:

- product name;
- broad category;
- flavor;
- ingredients;
- notable allergen/ingredient presence;
- maker/vendor;
- place of origin;
- freshness;
- preparation method;
- serving temperature;
- price;
- quality/reputation;
- whether a character has personally tried it;
- character preference.

Knowing one fact does not grant all others.

## Question intents that fair products should support where relevant

- What is this?
- What flavor is this?
- What's in it?
- Is it sweet / spicy / sour / hot / cold?
- How much is it?
- Who made it?
- Where did it come from?
- Is it fresh?
- Does it contain nuts or another notable ingredient?
- Have you tried it?
- Do you like it?
- What do you recommend?
- Why is it this color?
- Why is it moving?

Parser forms may vary; the authority is semantic rather than tied to one exact sentence pattern.

## Context-sensitive referents

Natural questions should be able to resolve recently salient products where parser authority can do so safely:

- `what flavor is this?`
- `what's in that?`
- `how much is it?`

`this`, `that`, and `it` must not become magical omniscient pronouns. Resolution should depend on ordinary parser focus / recent object context, and ambiguity should produce a useful clarification rather than a random guess.

## No omniscient NPCs

A character answers only from knowledge they plausibly possess.

Examples:

- the drink seller knows the drink;
- Mara can know what she has observed, smelled, tasted, bought, or been told;
- the fair manager may know the stall location and concession holder without knowing a secret recipe;
- a nearby child may know only that the drink is `the green one` and that they like it;
- an unrelated visitor may say they do not know and point toward the seller.

Correct ignorance is valid authored dialogue. Generic ignorance when the NPC should know is not.

## No generic-response escape hatch

For a named fair NPC, `I don't know anything about that` is not an acceptable default if the topic is:

- their own product;
- their own attraction;
- their own job;
- an event they just witnessed;
- an object they are visibly holding/using;
- a fact already established in conversation memory.

The content does not need an infinite answer surface. It needs deliberate bounded coverage of facts that the world itself invites the player to ask about.

## Knowledge acquisition

Meaningful facts may be learned through:

- direct examination;
- reading labels/signs/menus;
- asking a knowledgeable character;
- smelling;
- tasting/eating/drinking;
- observing preparation;
- witnessing a purchase or event;
- being explicitly told by Mara/NPC;
- prior fair memory.

A false or mistaken statement from an NPC should record **who claimed it**, not silently become objective world truth.

## Knowledge persistence

Meaningful product identification persists for the current play history and, where broader memory authority supports it, across later encounters.

Repeatedly asking a known question should not always replay first-contact prose. Follow-ups can be shorter, irritated, teasing, corrective, or memory-aware according to character.

## Qualification requirement

Eventually natural-play qualification must include at least:

1. encounter `large drink` without knowing its exact identity;
2. ask seller what flavor it is;
3. learn pear-lime identity;
4. ask Mara before and/or after she has enough evidence to know;
5. confirm a stranger does not receive omniscient recipe knowledge;
6. confirm the object remains the same physical object throughout identification.
