# Flathead Fair adult social, romance, and intimacy

**Status:** STABLE FOR PLANNING  
**Primary space:** `DANCE-PAVILION` with natural spillover to Observation Wheel, Pond Path and closing grounds  
**Evening service identity:** **The Lantern Table** — locked for planning

## Product law

The fair is not exclusively a child/family attraction. It is also an adult social place with dating, dancing, couples, flirting, quiet conversation, affection, evening food/drink, proposals, arguments, reconciliation, and established relationships.

The fair supplies **context**, not a relationship mode.

Broader relationship authority remains sovereign for consent, autonomy, rupture, privacy, intimacy and character-specific willingness.

There is no `ROMANTIC-ROOM` flag and no fair-specific intimacy minigame.

## The Lantern Table

### Identity

**The Lantern Table** is the public/common name of the fair's dedicated evening supper-and-drink service attached **inside the existing Dance Pavilion space**.

It is not a seventeenth major fairground room, not a separate tavern map, and not an age-gated nightclub.

Physically it is a pavilion-side evening service zone containing:

- a compact service counter;
- communal long tables;
- a few smaller edge tables;
- standing room near the music side of the pavilion;
- lamps/lanterns and ordinary pavilion lighting;
- clear paths between the dance floor, seating and exits.

The service area changes the **use** of `DANCE-PAVILION` at DUSK/EVENING without changing the locked 16-room geography.

### Operator

**Tomas Quince** is the named primary operator of The Lantern Table.

At DUSK, Tomas closes his ordinary Food Row cold-drink stand and shifts selected stock/equipment to the Dance Pavilion for evening service.

This planning decision supersedes only the older `Tomas Quince = Food Row through all of EVENING` placeholder in the core daily schedule. The broader phase law remains unchanged and should be consolidated into `FAIR_DAILY_OPERATIONS.md` before implementation decomposition.

No new named operator is required. Background concession help may exist as crowd/service texture without becoming persistent NPCs unless a later story needs one.

Tomas remains a bounded vendor:

- he knows his own evening menu, prices, stock and regular customers he actually remembers;
- he may recognize Lysa Marr, Tavin Roe, Mara or the Adventurer after real prior interactions;
- he is not a bartender-confessor who magically knows everyone's relationship history;
- he does not overhear every conversation simply because he runs the counter.

### Service hours

#### PREOPEN through AFTERNOON

The Lantern Table is not operating as an evening venue. The Dance Pavilion may still have seating, lessons, rehearsal or daytime program use.

#### DUSK

The service zone opens in stages:

- lamps are lit;
- Tomas moves into the pavilion and sets the counter;
- first hot/evening drinks become available;
- tables shift from passive daytime seating into active social/supper use;
- Orin Bell and musicians prepare nearby.

DUSK is an authored transition, not an instant room transformation.

#### EVENING

Full Lantern Table service is active alongside the Dance Pavilion program.

People may eat, drink, talk, watch dancing, dance, step away toward the wheel or Pond Path, return, or simply sit without purchasing anything.

#### CLOSING

Closing is sequential:

1. Tomas announces last paid orders before the final dance/program ends;
2. no new long food orders are accepted after that cutoff;
3. people with existing food/drink are not forced to discard it because the register closed;
4. Orin's final piece and the Lantern Table's final service overlap naturally;
5. lights/service reduce after the final piece and visitor departure begins;
6. Tomas closes/clears the counter and leaves through ordinary fair paths toward cleanup.

The venue does not remain as an automatic after-hours bar.

#### AFTER-HOURS

The Lantern Table is closed. Staff cleanup may continue where legitimate, but closed infrastructure is not converted into private date space merely because the crowd left.

### Alcohol boundary

The Lantern Table does **not** require an alcohol/intoxication system to function as an adult social venue.

The existing price book leaves the alcoholic/non-alcoholic identity of generic cider unresolved. Until broader product/intoxication authority exists, The Lantern Table's social identity comes from its evening hours, music, conversation and adult crowd shift, not from author-assumed drunkenness.

Do not silently introduce intoxication, age verification, hangovers or consent changes as side effects of this venue.

If broader authority later defines an alcoholic product, that product must compose ordinary consent/autonomy/safety law rather than becoming a fair-specific shortcut.

## Admission and purchase law

- Dance Pavilion entry remains **free** for the ordinary evening program unless a separately authored premium event later says otherwise.
- There is no drink minimum.
- Buying food/drink is optional.
- Prize tickets are not payment.
- Ride strips are not payment.
- Ordinary purchases use zorkmids.
- Nobody has to buy something to sit, listen, dance or accompany another person where seating/capacity permits.

The venue must remain enjoyable for a broke Adventurer.

## Social topology

The Dance Pavilion should contain a **gradient**, not binary `public/private` state.

### Counter / music-side standing area — highly public

- closest to service traffic and music;
- easy incidental conversation;
- low expectation of privacy;
- interruptions are plausible.

### Communal long tables — public/social

- strangers and recurring fairgoers can sit near one another;
- conversation can happen without every nearby NPC joining automatically;
- suitable for groups, food, watching the dance floor and casual introductions.

### Edge tables — quieter public

- somewhat removed from the main dance floor/counter;
- useful for a more focused conversation;
- still plainly inside a public pavilion;
- not soundproof, curtained or coded as intimacy rooms.

### Movement outward

If people actually want more privacy, the fair already has better physical contexts:

- Observation Wheel carriage;
- Pond Path;
- thinning closing grounds;
- leaving the fair for some broader legitimate private place.

The Lantern Table should therefore **not** steal every romantic/social function from the rest of the fair.

Orin Bell remains the Dance Pavilion bandleader. Tomas does not control the music and Orin does not run the concession.

Evening behavior can include:

- asking someone to dance;
- accepting/refusing;
- watching instead;
- sitting out a piece;
- requesting a song where Orin's repertoire supports it;
- eating/drinking between dances;
- stepping outside and returning;
- talking over quieter moments rather than during the loudest passage;
- leaving before closing.

Dancing is not a reward for buying dinner.

## Public affection and contextual reaction

Ordinary affection is not treated as scandalous merely because it is visible.

Context matters:

- holding hands at an edge table is ordinary;
- kissing briefly after a dance may be ordinary;
- obstructing the dance floor or a service counter may draw practical annoyance;
- louder/more disruptive behavior may draw attention for the disruption, not moral judgment;
- deeper intimacy belongs in contexts where broader privacy/consent authority genuinely supports it.

No fair-specific morality meter.

## Weather

The Dance Pavilion is a covered fallback social space, so ordinary rain may **increase** its social density rather than cancel the evening.

Weather can affect:

- crowd compression;
- whether outer edges remain comfortable;
- hot-drink demand;
- whether wheel/Pond Path alternatives remain attractive;
- music/performance relocation where broader weather authority supports it.

Severe conditions may close or evacuate the pavilion only through the real weather/safety authority. Do not create a second fair weather engine.

## Memory and records

The Lantern Table creates personal memories, not an institutional social log.

Possible personal memories:

- first dance;
- a particular song;
- who bought the cider;
- a conversation at an edge table;
- Lysa/Tavin being seen there;
- leaving the pavilion for the wheel;
- a closing-night disagreement or reconciliation.

These do **not** automatically enter the Fair Office or House of Records.

A public performance program or formally documented incident may independently create a record through existing record authority. The private meaning of the event does not.
