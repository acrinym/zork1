# Flathead Fair products and zorkmid economy

**Status:** DESIGNING

## Currency law

Normal fair commerce uses **zorkmids (`zm`)**. Midway prize tickets/chits may exist as a separate reward currency, but tickets never replace ordinary money for food, normal merchandise, or services.

The fair should become the first strong practical purchasing-power reference for the broader Highly Extended Zork economy without becoming a full economic simulation.

## Working purchasing-power bands

These are planning targets, not yet final canon:

- `1 zm`: trivial everyday purchase;
- `2–5 zm`: ordinary snack/drink or small fair purchase;
- `6–20 zm`: meaningful small discretionary purchase;
- `20–75 zm`: good clothing, gear, gift, or artisan item;
- `75–250 zm`: expensive specialty/artisan purchase;
- `500+ zm`: major luxury or serious equipment rather than routine fair spending.

Prices must be calibrated against existing money/treasure authorities before lock.

## Product families

### Food and drink

Elephant ears, candy apples, roasted nuts, pies, pastries, fried food, sausages or local equivalents, fruit, sweets, cider/lemonade/fizz, souvenir cups, specialty regional foods.

### Clothing and wearable souvenirs

Ribbons, caps, scarves, shirts, festival clothing, artisan pieces, annual commemorative items. These should compose wardrobe authority rather than creating fair-only clothing logic.

### Toys and souvenirs

Stuffed grues, carved creatures, miniature landmarks, wind-up nonsense, toy lamps/mailboxes, fair ribbons, commemorative pieces.

### Crafts

Pottery, glass, woodwork, textiles, leather goods, metalwork, art, candles, jewelry, baskets, tools.

### Books and paper goods

Fair guide, programs, local histories, maps, cookbooks, fishing guides, songbooks, propaganda, pamphlets, used books.

### Fishing goods

Bait, hooks, lures, line, rod rental, rods, containers and derby supplies.

### Agricultural / market goods

Produce, preserves, honey, spices, flowers, seeds, crafts, and other products only if supported by final fair lore.

### Curiosities

Puzzle boxes, old coins, strange mechanisms, dubious magical objects, fossils/minerals, unidentified junk, unusual keys, lenses, and items whose significance may emerge much later.

## Price philosophy

Do not inflate prices merely because this is a game. A five-zorkmid snack should feel meaningfully cheaper than a fifty-zorkmid artisan gift. Expensive does not mean emotionally superior: Mara or another NPC may genuinely prefer a tiny cheap object.

## Commerce agency

Mara and NPCs may buy things for themselves. The Adventurer is not the sole purchasing authority in the world.
